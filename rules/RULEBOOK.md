# DECCAN

**2 to 8 players · about 20 minutes · 72 cards**

Two armies form on one field. You do not command one of them — you put your own units into one,
alongside rivals who are doing the same, and you are paid for what your units kill. The army is
not your side. It is the crowd you are standing in.

**Seven kills wins.** At every player count.

---

## THE WORDS

Six words carry rules. Everywhere they appear in this book they mean exactly this.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **VALUE** | The number printed on a unit. It is what the unit **deals**, what it can **survive**, and — inverted — **when it acts**. One number, three jobs. |
| **BAND** | Every unit of the same value. A charge resolves band by band, lowest value first, and everything in a band strikes at the same instant. |
| **THE CHARGE** | The battle. Both armies turn face up and resolve at once. Only a senior partner may call one. |
| **STAND** | A unit that survives a charge stays on the field, **face up**, undamaged. |
| **RECOVER** | A unit that leaves the field — killed, withdrawn, or sent home by a Spy — returns to its owner's hand and cannot be committed again for one full lap of the table. |
| **THE LINE** | An army is a **line, not a pile**. Units are laid left to right in the order they were committed and are never reordered. The leftmost card has been standing longest. |
| **LEFTMOST WINS** | **The one tie-breaker in the game.** Whenever two units are equally eligible for anything — taking a blow, taking the credit for a kill, being named by a Spy — the **leftmost** is chosen. You never need to remember anything: look at the table. |
| **SENIOR PARTNER** | The player holding the most value standing in an army. Only they may call the charge. |

## THE CARDS

**Eight units. Five bodies and three that do something.**

| Unit | Value | |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| **Elephant** | 6 | |
| **Cannon** | 5 | |
| **Rifleman** | 4 | |
| **Warrior** | 3 | |
| **Horseman** | 2 | The horse is quick and thin — it strikes before the foot, and dies to less. |
| **Commander** | 1 | **Strikes for the value of the highest enemy unit on the field.** |
| **Spy** | 1 | **One enemy unit leaves without a fight.** No choice. The Spy stays. |
| **Scout** | 1 | Deploys **face up**. **Look** at one face-down enemy unit. It stays face down. |

**Value is agility, inverted.** The three 1s act first, then the Horseman, then the Warrior, up to
the Elephant. Nothing in the game acts before a 1, so the three ability cards always get their text
off — and the Elephant, which acts last, may never swing at all.

**Three verbs, and they do not overlap: the Commander hits, the Spy removes, the Scout looks.**

### The Commander
It copies the **blow**, not the body. Its durability stays 1 — anything kills it. It copies the
**enemy's** best, not its own army's, and **Commanders do not copy Commanders**, or two facing each
other would define one another. Facing nothing but Commanders it strikes for 1.

Because it reads the field *after* the Spies have fired, a Spy that pulls out the enemy Elephant
also shrinks the enemy Commander's blow.

### The Spy
It names the highest-value enemy unit. That unit **goes home and recovers**. It does not strike, it
cannot be killed, and **nobody scores a point for it**. Two Spies may not name the same unit.

The Spy is not spent doing this. It stays and fights on.

**Spies facing each other do not cancel out.** Each names the *highest* enemy unit, which is almost
never the other Spy — so both Elephants walk home and the two Spies are left standing in an empty
field, killing each other for a point apiece.

### The Scout
It goes out face up, so the whole table knows you scouted. **What you saw is yours** — the card you
looked at stays face down for everyone else. Then it stands there where everybody can see that you
know something they do not.

> ⚠️ **The Scout is decided but not yet built.** The model still deploys it face down and gives it a
> public **reveal** — the card turns face up for the whole table — rather than a private **look**.
> Those are different cards: a reveal buys something for everyone and is paid for by one person.
> Nothing about the Scout should be quoted from a measurement until this is fixed.

---

## SETUP

* Each player takes a **ruler**: a fixed deck of **nine units**, held in hand.
* Put out two army areas. **Each army holds at most four units, from at most four players.**
* Youngest player begins. Play passes left.

---

## A TURN

On your turn take **exactly one** action.

**DEPLOY** — put one unit from your hand **face down** into an army, **at the right-hand end of the
line**. *(A Scout deploys face up.)* You may only ever stand in **one** army: once you have a unit
in an army, every later unit goes to the same one.

**WITHDRAW** — take one of **your** units off the field. It recovers. This is what un-jams a full
line, and it costs a turn and nothing else.

**DEFECT** — move your whole contingent to the other army. ⚠️ **Only legal when a single unit is
all you have standing**, so crossing takes preparation: withdraw down to one, sit through its
recovery, then go. An ally thinning out is visibly getting ready to leave.

**CALL THE CHARGE** — only the **senior partner** of an army, and only when **both armies hold at
least one unit**. You cannot charge at nobody.

**PASS** — do nothing.

**If both armies are full, the charge happens immediately, whether anyone wants it or not.** That
is what stops the game dying of hesitation. If the table passes round and round with nobody willing
to act, the game ends where it stands.

---

## THE CHARGE

**1. Reveal.** Every face-down unit in both armies turns face up.

**2. The Spies fire.** Each Spy names the highest-value enemy unit still unnamed. Those units leave
the field and recover. They never fought, and no one is paid for them.

**3. The bands resolve, lowest value first.**

Everything printed 1 strikes, then everything printed 2, and so on up to 6. **Within a band,
everything strikes at the same instant.** After a band resolves, anything that has taken damage
equal to its value is dead.

**A unit killed in an earlier band never strikes.**

Each striker puts its blow into **the enemy unit it can finish**; if it can finish none, into **the
biggest one still standing**. Ties go **leftmost**. Damage stacks across bands — two Warriors
together finish a Cannon neither could finish alone.

**A big unit usually never gets to choose.** A Cannon 5 facing a Horseman 2 and a Warrior 3 takes
2 and then 3 and is dead at band 3, before band 5 comes round. Under this ladder, the question of
what an Elephant would have aimed at rarely arises.

**4. Count the kills.** For each unit that died, work out who takes the credit — **one point, to one
player, and it cannot be split.**

> **a. The single largest blow takes it.** A Rifleman 4 standing at the back of the line beats a
> Horseman 2 at the front, because 4 is bigger than 2.
>
> **b. If the largest blows tie, the leftmost takes it** — the unit that has been standing in the
> line longest.

⚠️ **THIS IS FIRST TO COMMIT, NOT FIRST TO STRIKE.** Units of the same value are the same band and
strike **at the same instant** — there is no first blow and no last blow to point at. The tie is
broken by something that happened turns earlier, when the card was put down. That is the whole
reason an army is laid out as a line: *who committed first* is just **look at the table**.

**Three Horsemen kill an Elephant and only one of them is paid.** 2 + 2 + 2 is exactly 6, so all
three blows are needed and all three tie — the leftmost Horseman's owner scores, and the other two
players spent a card for nothing. **You fight together and you are scored separately.** Somebody has
to commit first, everyone can see who has not, and the front of the line is worth having.

**5. The Scouts look.**

**6. The field — but only if the charge drew blood.** ⚠️ **If nothing died, nobody takes the
field.** Otherwise the army with more surviving value takes it. It scores nothing at all — the
field is worth only the turn that comes with it. If the values tie, nobody takes it.

*Why the blood condition exists: after a charge the front is quiet by construction, so a second
charge straight away kills nothing. Without this, the caller's side would still take the field on
surviving value, hand the turn back to its own senior partner, and they could do it again — and
again, for ever.*

**7. Sort out the survivors.** The dead go home and recover. **Survivors stand, face up, whole** —
damage does not carry between charges. A unit that is not killed is not hurt.

Survivors **keep their place in the line**. Gaps close up without reshuffling, and anything committed
afterwards goes to the right of them — so a veteran of the last charge outranks every fresh card.

**8. Work out seniority again, then play on.** With the dead cleared, each army's senior partner is
whoever now holds the most value standing in it. **The senior partner of the army that took the
field takes the next turn**; if nobody took the field, play simply continues in order.

⚠️ **THE PERSON WHO STARTS IS OFTEN NOT THE PERSON WHO CALLED THE CHARGE.** Seniority is recounted
on the survivors, so calling a charge that kills your own biggest unit hands the next turn — and
the army — to somebody else. Measured over 14,664 charges, seniority changed hands in **41%** of
the charges that had a winner.

---

## SCORING

**One point for every enemy unit your units killed. Seven points wins.**

Nothing else scores. Not holding the field, not surviving, not the size of what you killed — an
Elephant and a Scout are each worth exactly one point.

**A charge can carry two players past seven at once, and they share the win.** It is not a freak
result — measured over 9,000 games it happens **11.5%** of the time, because a single charge pays
several players and the seventh point is often not the last one scored. If your table wants a
single winner, break it on **who holds the field**.

---

## SENIORITY

The first player to put a unit into an empty army is its senior partner. After that it is whoever
holds the **most value standing** in it; on a tie the incumbent keeps it. Lose everything and the
army has no senior partner until someone deploys.

The senior partner chooses **when** the charge happens — for everybody, including the three players
whose units are standing in the line beside theirs.

## WHY IT IS BUILT THIS WAY

**Everything recovers. Nothing is destroyed.** A killed unit goes home, waits a lap, and comes back.
The cost of losing is tempo, not material — which is what lets a 20-minute game contain seven kills
and still have every deck intact at the end.

**After a charge, the front is quiet by construction.** Every survivor stands facing units it could
not finish, or it would have. Nothing more dies until somebody commits a fresh card. **So every
deployment is a deliberate act of violence**, and withdrawing is a real move rather than a
concession.

**Timing is the game.** Charge now against a thin line for a small harvest, or wait while both sides
thicken and the killing gets bigger — knowing the enemy is arming too, and that whoever calls it
chooses the moment for everyone.

**You are standing next to your rivals.** Your army is not your team. Kills are scored by the player
who landed the blow, so the four of you in one line are competing for the same corpses.

## WHAT IS NOT SETTLED

* **The roster.** The eight real rulers have not been searched against these cards. This is the
  largest open item in the game.
* **The Scout.** Decided as a private *look*; the model still implements a public *reveal*.
* **What an ability costs.** The roster search prices Spy, Scout and Commander at their printed 1 —
  one *less* than the cheapest fighter — so an ability is a discount, and the box has to be stocked
  by a quota rather than by the card being worth taking. That is a pricing problem and it is open.
* **Two players.** Seven kills heads-up runs longer per person than at any other count.
* **Whether anyone will ever gang up.** Three Horsemen are needed to kill an Elephant and only one
  of the three is paid. That is either the central tension of a game where you fight together and
  score apart, or the reason cheap units never actually combine and the whole band ladder never
  fires at a table. **A simulation cannot answer it** — bots do not mind being used. Play it.
