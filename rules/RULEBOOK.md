# DECCAN

**2–8 players · 30–45 minutes · 79 cards**

Two armies form on one piece of ground. You do not command an army — you put units *into* one,
alongside whoever else wants the same ground, and it pays out to whoever is still standing when
the dust settles.

Every unit you commit to a losing army is gone for good. Win, and your units come home to fight
again. Lose, and a **Power Broker** comes to you instead — so the players who are behind are the
only ones who ever get the game's best cards.

---

## Components

**Eight rulers**, each with a **FORCE** of eight units. Every unit shows an **ARM** and a printed
**STRENGTH**. No two rulers hold the same eight cards, and every ruler holds at least one of
every arm — so every ruler can answer every arm.

**Fifteen Power Brokers**, three of each of five kinds, shuffled face down into a **supply**.
If the supply ever empties, shuffle the discarded brokers to make a new one.

## Setup

Each player takes a ruler and holds their whole Force. Shuffle the supply. Choose a start player.

| Players | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Points to win** | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |

## The ring

> ### ELEPHANT → RIFLEMAN → CANNON → HORSEMAN → WARRIOR → back to ELEPHANT
>
> **Each arm cancels the next two along the ring.** It is cancelled by the two behind it.

| | cancels | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Elephant 9** | Rifleman *(trampled before they reload)* | Cannon *(the battery overrun)* |
| **Cannon 7** | Horseman *(grapeshot into cavalry)* | Warrior *(shreds massed foot)* |
| **Horseman 5** | Warrior *(ridden down in the open)* | Elephant *(light horse outpaces them)* |
| **Rifleman 3** | Cannon *(gun crews shot down)* | Horseman *(a volley stops a charge)* |
| **Warrior 1** | Elephant *(brought down at close quarters)* | Rifleman *(closed with before reload)* |

**A Warrior 1 cancels an Elephant 9.** The cheapest card in the game answers the dearest — the
number on a card says how much it is worth *if it survives*, never whether it will.

## A round

### 1 · Muster

Beginning with the start player and going clockwise, on your turn you **COMMIT** one unit face
down to one of the two armies, or you **pass**. Turns keep going round the table until every
player passes in succession.

* There are only ever **two armies**, and each holds **three units and at most three players**.
* The first player to commit to an army is its **LEADER**. To commit into an army you are not
  already in, offer the unit to its leader, who accepts or refuses **without looking at it**.
  Once you are in an army, you reinforce it freely.
* **A refusal does not end your turn.** Offer to the other army instead, or open it yourself.
  You may offer to each army once per turn. Refused by both, you pass.
* You cannot hold units in both armies.
* Passing is not folding. You may commit on a later turn.

### 2 · Charge

Turn every committed unit face up.

### 3 · Battle

**First the ring, then the abilities, then the count.**

> **Every unit cancels one unit** of an arm it beats, in the other army — the **strongest** one
> it can reach. Work all of this out from the armies as revealed: a unit that is itself
> cancelled still cancels.

**A cancelled unit does nothing.** It adds no strength, and its ability never happens.

Then every surviving **Power Broker** resolves its **ON REVEAL** ability, and each army totals the
strength of its survivors. **The higher total takes the GROUND.** Level totals, and both armies take it.

### 4 · Spoils

**The army that took the ground pays ONE POINT to its largest contributor — the player who
committed the most units to it. Tied contributors each take one.**

Contribution counts what you **committed**, not what survived. A unit the ring cancelled still
counts toward the point, which is why sending your best into a contested ground can pay even
when it dies.

Then settle the armies:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Took the ground** | Your units **RECOVER** — set them aside, and take them back into your hand after the next round. |
| **Did not** | Your units are **gone for good**, and you **recruit one Power Broker** from the supply — one per defeated player, whatever you committed. |

A Power Broker is a unit in every way: it has an arm, it fights at its strength, and it can be
cancelled. A recruited broker that is later lost leaves the game; the supply is never refilled.

Pass the start player to the left.

---

## Winning

The first player to reach the target wins. If two reach it in the same round, they share it.

If two rounds pass in a row with nothing committed, or nobody has a unit left to commit, the
game ends and the most points wins.

## Power Brokers

| | | | |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| **Siege Elephant** | 10 | ON DEPLOY | Deploy **face up**. Look at one enemy unit — it stays face up. |
| **Sultan Rockets** | 8 | ON DEFEAT | Burn the winning army's recovering units. |
| **Spy** | 6 | ON REVEAL | **Exchange** with the enemy's strongest unit. **Permanent** — each card joins the other Force for good. |
| **Sepoy** | 4 | ON REVEAL | **Alone** in your army: double strength. |
| **Subhedar** | 2 | ON REVEAL | Remove the enemy's weakest unit. |

**Every broker says when it acts.**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **ON DEPLOY** | When you commit it, before anything is revealed. **The ring cannot stop it** — the card has not been contested yet. |
| **ON REVEAL** | The abilities step of the battle. The ring resolves first, so **only survivors act**. |
| **ON DEFEAT** | After the ground is decided, if the card survived the ring and its army did not take the ground. |

Only the Siege Elephant is ON DEPLOY, and it is face up precisely because it acts before the
ring. Every other ability needs its card to survive the ring first.

**ON REVEAL is not the Charge.** Both armies turn face up at the Charge, but nothing resolves
until the ring has been read off them.

A Sepoy's **alone** is fixed the moment the armies turn face up: it counts the units revealed
beside it, so an army-mate that the ring cancels still counts as company.

**A borrowed unit never earns the alone bonus.** A Sepoy that changed hands through a Spy's
exchange fights at its printed strength for its new army, however alone it is left there.

---

## Three things worth knowing

**Your best card is the first one shot at.** A cancelling unit always takes the strongest thing
it can reach, so an Elephant draws fire that a Warrior never would.

**Losing is how you get stronger.** Every defeat hands you a Power Broker, and only the defeated
ever hold one. The leader is the one player who cannot buy the game's sharpest cards.

**However many you hold of your best arm, you still hold one of every other.** You always have
an answer to everything — and so does everyone else. Most rulers also carry the arm that answers
their own: the Sultan's elephants are cancelled by warriors, so the Sultan fields warriors.

## Glossary

**ARM** — Elephant, Cannon, Horseman, Rifleman or Warrior. Printed on every unit.

**CANCEL** — take a unit out of the battle. It adds no strength and its ability does not happen.

**COMMIT** — place a unit face down into an army. It cannot be taken back.

**FORCE** — the eight units a ruler starts with.

**GROUND** — what the two armies are fighting over. Taken by the higher surviving total.

**ON DEPLOY / ON REVEAL / ON DEFEAT** — when a Power Broker's ability happens. On deployment,
before the ring and safe from it · at the abilities step, survivors only · after the ground is
decided, for a surviving card whose army lost.

**LEADER** — the first player to commit to an army. Accepts or refuses every unit offered to it,
without looking.

**POWER BROKER** — an earned unit. Recruited only by losing a battle.

**RECOVER** — set aside after a victory, and taken back into hand after the next round.

**STRENGTH** — the number on a unit. It never changes.
