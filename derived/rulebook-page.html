DECCAN

2–8 players30–45 minutes76 cards

Two armies form on one piece of ground. You do not command an army — you put units into one, alongside whoever else wants the same ground, and it pays out to whoever is still standing when the dust settles.

Every unit you commit to a losing army is gone for good. Win, and your units come home to fight again. Lose, and a Power Broker comes to you instead — so the players who are behind are the only ones who ever get the game’s best cards.

The cheapest card in the game answers the dearest. The number on a card says how much it is worth if it survives — never whether it will.

A round

1

Muster

Clockwise from the start player, commit one unit face down to one of the two armies, or pass. Turns keep going round until everyone passes in succession.

  • Two armies only. Each holds three units and at most three players.
  • First into an army is its leader. To join an army you are not already in, offer the unit — the leader accepts or refuses without looking. Once in, you reinforce freely.
  • A refusal does not end your turn. Offer to the other army, or open it yourself. One offer per army per turn.
  • Never both armies. Passing is not folding.
2

Charge

Turn every committed unit face up.

3

Battle

First the ring, then the abilities, then the count.

  • Every unit cancels one unit of an arm it beats — the strongest it can reach.
  • Work it out from the armies as revealed: a unit that is itself cancelled still cancels.
  • A cancelled unit does nothing — no strength, and its ability never happens.
  • Surviving Power Brokers resolve, then total the survivors. The higher total takes the ground; level totals, and both armies take it.
4

Spoils

Each player in an army that took the ground scores one point for every surviving unit of theirs. Then settle the armies and pass the start player left.

After the battleWhat happens to your units
Took the groundThey recover — back in your hand after the next round.
Did notThey are gone for good, and you recruit one Power Broker — one per defeated player, whatever you committed.

The eight rulers

Seven units each

A specialist takes three of one arm. A pair takes two of its arm and two of the next arm round the ring. Everyone holds at least one of every arm, so every ruler can answer everything.

The supply

Twenty Power Brokers, recruited only by losing

Four of each. A Power Broker is a unit in every way — it has an arm, it fights at its strength, and it can be cancelled. Only the Siege Elephant’s look survives being cancelled, because it happens as you deploy.

First to the target wins; two reaching it in the same round share it. If two rounds pass with nothing committed, or nobody has a unit left to commit, the game ends and the most points wins.

Three things worth knowing

Your best card is the first one shot at.

A cancelling unit always takes the strongest thing it can reach, so an Elephant draws fire that a Warrior never would.

Losing is how you get stronger.

Every defeat hands you a Power Broker, and only the defeated ever hold one. The leader is the one player who cannot buy the game’s sharpest cards.

A ruler with three of one arm still holds one of every other.

However hard you lean on your speciality, you always have an answer to everything — and so does everyone else.

Glossary

Arm
Elephant, Cannon, Horseman, Rifleman or Warrior. Printed on every unit.
Cancel
Take a unit out of the battle. It adds no strength and its ability does not happen.
Commit
Place a unit face down into an army. It cannot be taken back.
Force
The seven units a ruler starts with.
Ground
What the two armies are fighting over. Taken by the higher surviving total.
Leader
The first player to commit to an army. Accepts or refuses every unit offered to it, without looking.
Power Broker
An earned unit. Recruited only by losing a battle.
Recover
Set aside after a victory, and taken back into hand after the next round.
Strength
The number on a unit. It never changes.
