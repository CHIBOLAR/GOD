DECCAN

2–8 players30–45 minutes108 cards

Two armies form on one piece of ground, three fronts wide. You do not command an army — you put units into one, alongside whoever else wants the same ground, and it pays out to whoever actually won the fighting. Sometimes that is you. Sometimes you have just spent three good units buying somebody else a victory.

Every unit you commit is gone for good, win or lose. Your faction is the whole of your war.

Horse rides down guns · guns break foot · foot holds horse. A countered unit loses its front whatever its strength.

Components

Nine factions of twelve units

Every unit shows a type — horse, foot or guns — and a printed strength from 1 to 9.

Every faction holds the same twelve numbers and four units of each type. What differs is which type each number is printed on: the Marathas’ nines are cavalry, the Siddis’ are guns.

The ground has three fronts — Van, Centre and Rear — and a row for each of the two armies.

A round

1

Muster

Beginning with the start player and going clockwise, on your turn you commit one unit face down into an empty slot, or you pass. Turns keep going round the table until every player passes in succession.

  • There are only ever two armies, and each has one slot per front — three units, and so at most three players.
  • The first player to commit to an army is its leader. To commit into an army you are not already in, offer the unit to its leader, who accepts or refuses without looking at it. Once you are in, you reinforce freely.
  • A refusal does not end your turn. Offer to the other army instead, or open it yourself. One offer to each army per turn. Refused by both, you pass.
  • You cannot hold units in both armies. Passing is not folding.
2

Charge

Turn every committed unit face up.

3

Battle

Settle each front on its own, in this order:

  1. Countered? That unit loses, whatever its strength.
  2. Otherwise the higher strength wins the front.
  3. Equal strength — nobody wins the front.
  4. An empty slot loses to any unit.
4

Spoils

Score, then every committed unit is spent — laid face up in front of its owner, out of the game for good. Win or lose. Pass the start player to the left.

Each player in the army that took the ground scores one point for every front they personally won.

The beaten army scores nothing, and a ground nobody takes pays nobody.

Four things worth knowing

One unit cannot take a contested ground.

One front is never a majority of three. A single unit is an opening for others to join, a bid for the Centre, or a free point if nobody bothers to contest you.

Two units is a real army.

Win both fronts you contest and the ground is yours — two cards spent for two points, where three cards buy three. Neither is right. Certainty costs cards.

Sitting out costs nothing but the round.

Every unit you commit is gone forever, so the player who wins most is the player who runs dry first.

Everybody can see what your faction cannot do.

The Firangi have no cavalry and the table knows it. What they cannot see is which unit you just put on the Van.

Glossary

Army
One of the two sides forming on the ground. Three slots, one per front.
Commit
Place a unit face down in an empty slot. It cannot be taken back.
Counter
Horse beats guns, guns beat foot, foot beats horse. A countered unit loses its front however strong it is.
Faction
The twelve units you start with. When they are gone you are done committing.
Front
Van, Centre or Rear. Each is settled on its own.
Ground
What the two armies are fighting over. Won by taking more fronts, or by taking the Centre when the fronts are level.
Leader
The first player to commit to an army. Accepts or refuses every unit offered to it, without looking.
Spent
Committed, and therefore out of the game for good, whatever the result.
Strength
The number printed on a unit. It never changes.
